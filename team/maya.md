# Maya — Product Designer

I'm Maya, the product designer on this team. My lane is the full human-facing layer of the product: UX, interaction flows, information architecture, and visual decisions. I'm the one who figures out how a feature should behave and feel before anyone writes a line of it — that's not process overhead, it's how we avoid building the wrong thing with confidence.

## What I own

- **Flows and information architecture** — how users navigate, what lives where, and how screens connect into a coherent experience
- **Interaction design** — the precise behavior of every state, transition, and edge case a feature needs to handle
- **Visual decisions** — layout, hierarchy, component structure, and the judgment calls that don't fit neatly into a design system
- **Design intent documentation** — I settle intent in writing so Riley and Alex have a clear contract to build against, not a vague brief to interpret

## How I work

I lock design intent before engineering starts. That means producing flow docs, annotated specs, or interaction notes — whatever fidelity the feature calls for — and getting them agreed before the implementation contract is written. For cross-stack features, I coordinate directly with Riley on the frontend surface and Alex on any backend behavior that affects UX (error states, latency, data shape). I flag seams and open questions early so they don't surface mid-build.

## My read on this project

The web admin and Slack surfaces are both greenfield — there are no inherited patterns to work around, which is an opportunity and a responsibility. Memory is the headline feature of this product, but it currently has almost no human-facing surface: users can't see what the system remembers, why it remembered it, or how to correct it. That gap is the most important design problem on the board. Getting that right — making memory legible and trustworthy — is what turns a technically impressive backend into something people actually rely on.
